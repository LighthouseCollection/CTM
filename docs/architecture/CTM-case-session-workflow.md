## 1. Initial completeness review

After a formal application submission, the council administrator or secretary reviews it for completeness. These are the initial reviewers; do not assign initial review to an investigating member automatically. Review may accept the application or reject it and send it back to the applicant. “Rejected and returned” here describes initial completeness review, not a council ruling on the petition's merits, an issued decree, case closure or deletion.

Application acceptance permits presentation as a new case at an official council meeting. It is not a decree, determination of grounds or permission to remarry. Required automatic upload safety remains separate: files do not acquire a new manual council file-acceptance gate.

Record each review action, actual administrator/secretary actor, server timestamp and the exact application revision/document manifest reviewed. Preserve the initial submitted application and every returned/corrected submission history. Confirmed: a returned application retains its case number and application identity. The applicant may correct and resubmit without paying again. Preserve the original submission and all subsequent corrections, file versions and resubmissions with the actual actor and server timestamp. Bind each resubmission to its exact answer revision/document manifest and the existing paid/waived obligation; do not create a new case, new initial filing fee or deletable draft. Preserve the original attestation; the previously confirmed no-re-sign correction policy remains. The review uses Accept / Reject actions. Reject requires a nonblank textarea explanation identifying what the applicant must correct; the explanation is visible to that applicant and retained with the review action. Reject returns the application for correction. Precise returned-state lifecycle and treatment of edits during review remain to be agreed. A return does not itself imply final merits rejection, closure or release of the existing open-case claim.

## 2. Official council meeting: two ordered parts

The official council meeting includes the council president and all council members. Attendance requirements/quorum and whether every member must physically be present are not specified by this description.

### Part 1: new-case presentation and assignment

Accepted new cases are presented to the council president and council members. Each case is briefed and assigned to one council member. Part 1 handles assignment of the new cases before the session moves to ongoing-case hearings.

Keep the case, session, agenda position, briefing record/reference, actual assignment action/actor/time and assigned member linked. Preserve assignment history and the one-current-assignment constraint. The council president selects the assigned member during the official meeting. An active administrator or secretary records that assignment in the system. Preserve the president who made the assignment decision separately from the administrator/secretary who entered it, along with the session, assigned member, decision time and recording time. Recording the assignment does not grant administrator/secretary authority to choose the member independently. All authorized council members still retain case read access.

### Part 2: hearings for ongoing cases

The second part hears ongoing cases. The currently assigned council member presents the case. Record the relevant case/session/agenda relationship, actual presenting member and hearing record through the later hearing data design. This does not require every ongoing case to be heard at every session or imply an automatic calendar assignment.

The detailed hearing procedure, scheduling, attendees, recommendation/decision/vote handling, minutes approval and decree consequences remain for later discussion. No child testimony is introduced.


### Agenda preparation, member requests and carry-forward

The administrator or secretary prepares the official meeting agenda: accepted new cases for assignment in Part 1 and ongoing cases scheduled for presentation/hearing in Part 2. Each active council member may mark their currently assigned case or cases for presentation at an upcoming session. Record the requesting member, case, requested session and server timestamp; distinguish the member's request from the prepared agenda placement. A member's assignment does not grant management of the whole session agenda.

If session time runs out before a scheduled case is presented, carry that case forward to the next session's agenda. Preserve the original agenda entry as not presented because time ran out and link the next-session entry to it. Preserve each carry-forward event, source and destination sessions, actor or system origin and timestamp. Do not overwrite the original session association or count the unpresented item as a completed hearing. Carrying a case to another session does not change its assigned council member. When administrator or secretary closes the session, automatically carry unpresented cases forward if the next council session has an identified date. If no dated next session exists, closure is still permitted: the unpresented cases have no upcoming session assignment and remain awaiting agenda placement. Once a session date is defined, administrator or secretary manually assigns those cases to its agenda; creating the date alone does not automatically place them. Preserve the original unpresented agenda entries, closing actor, automatic carry-forward events where applicable and later manual placement actor/time. Never invent a destination date or change the assigned council member.


### Presentation outcomes and applicant updates

Administrator or secretary records the outcome of each case presentation during the meeting, linked to the case, session and actual presentation. An outcome may be next steps or recommendations without a decision; do not require a decree or final ruling for every presentation. Preserve the recorded outcome, recording actor and timestamp. The complete outcome catalog and any later editing/approval rules remain to be discussed.

Confirmed example: the case was presented, no decision was made and further information is needed. Record that result and the next steps/recommendations without closing the case or inventing a final determination. Notify the applicant that the case was presented and no decision has been made pending further information being gathered. This applicant update must not expose council-only findings, notes or deliberations. Recording the presentation outcome triggers an automated applicant-facing message appropriate to that outcome. Administrator, secretary or the assigned case council member may optionally add a message to the applicant; a separate manually written update is not required. Keep the optional applicant message distinct from council-only outcome details, recommendations, findings and deliberations. Preserve the triggering outcome record, automated message/template version, optional text, actual recording actor, recipient and delivery history. On recording the presentation outcome, immediately create the applicant portal notification and queue the email for delivery, using the applicant notification language. These outcome updates do not use the 10-minute applicant-edit digest. Preserve delivery attempts and avoid duplicate notifications for a retried recording operation.


Clarification: next steps and recommendations may be recorded by administrator, secretary or the currently assigned case council member. The same interface provides separate rich-text areas for internal information and applicant-facing communication. Internal text is council-only and is never copied into the applicant notification. No structured requirement to select who must supply further information is introduced: necessary instructions may be written in the rich text.

For a pending case presentation, the applicant-facing area contains the default message: "Your case was presented on {presentation_date}. Your case is still pending." Additional applicant-facing text may be provided by any of those three authorized roles. The applicant receives the public message immediately through the portal and email in their selected language. This expands authority for next steps/recommendations and the accompanying applicant message; it does not establish new authority to issue final decisions or decrees. While the case is open, administrator, secretary or the currently assigned council member may correct saved next steps and recommendations. Preserve every revision, its actual editor and server timestamp; never overwrite the earlier record. Existing closed-case read-only rules remain. Previously delivered applicant messages remain preserved as sent. Corrections to saved next steps/recommendations send no notification. The original presentation-triggered applicant message is an outgoing communication in the existing case message history, not a separate correspondence subsystem. Record its originating presentation/outcome, actual actor, applicant recipient, exact sent content, timestamp and portal/email delivery history. Corrections do not resend or alter that delivered message; subsequent applicant communications use the existing authorized case messaging process.
