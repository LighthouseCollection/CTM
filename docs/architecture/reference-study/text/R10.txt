PART II - MARITAL HISTORY – ASSESSMENT 
 
A declaration of nullity  is not a divorce, but an ecclesiastical study of the sacramental bond  to 
determine, with moral certainty  (cf. Dignitas Connubii , art. 247), whether a valid marriage was ever 
established at the time of consent. Marriage comes into being through the free and mutual act of consent, 
when both parties knowingly say “yes” to one another ( can. 1057 §2 ). This consent must include proper 
intention, awareness, and interior freedom ( can. 1055 §1; can. 1095 ). At times, internal or external factors 
may impair these essential elements. For this reason, the Tribunal first identifies the possible grounds of 
nullity to understand the foundation of the relationship and examine the validity of the sacrament, always 
mindful of the words of Christ: “What God has joined together, let no one separate” (Mt 19:6). 
 
When it becomes evident that the unity and permanence intended by God were never truly realized, 
the Church, exercising her pastoral and judicial mission ( cf. Mitis Iudex Dominus Iesus , Preamble), may 
declare the marriage invalid and assist the parties in finding healing and renewed participation in the 
sacramental life. This process, always guided by justice and mercy, marks the beginning of the Pathway to 
Matrimonial Healing. The following assessment, held in strict confidentiality, helps the Tribunal understand 
the personal and relational circumstances of both parties. Please review each statement carefully and mark 
those that apply to the Petitioner (YOU) and/or the Respondent (RESP). There are no right or wrong 
answers—only your sincere reflection.

By my signature below, I respectfully request that the Metropolitan Tribunal of the Archdiocese of 
Boston examine my marriage with the RESPONDENT, in order that it be declared null and void on the ground of 
a defect of matrimonial consent, rather than on the basis of a defect of canonical form or the existence of a 
diriment impediment (cf. can. 1095–1107). 
DECLARATION AND UNDERSTANDING OF THE PETITIONER 
- I AFFIRM that the testimony I offer in this case is truthful and corresponds faithfully to the facts as 
they actually occurred (cf. can. 1532; Dignitas Connubii, art. 163). 
- I AGREE  to provide, at the proper time and upon request of the Tribunal, all relevant documents, 
proofs, and witnesses necessary for the just adjudication of the case (cf. can. 1526 §1; Dignitas Connubii, art. 
167). 
- I UNDERSTAND  that the RESPONDENT will be cited according to law and that the information 
provided will be made known to him/her, in conformity with the principle of legitimate defense and the right of 
both parties to be heard (cf. can. 1508; Dignitas Connubii, arts. 56–58). 
FURTHER DECLARATIONS 
- I CONFIRM my full understanding that the process of investigating the validity of a marriage bond is a 
serious judicial procedure intended to serve the truth of the sacred bond and the good of souls, and that the 
Metropolitan Tribunal shall not be influenced by any external pressure or expectations regarding the time 
required for due process (cf. Mitis Iudex, preamble III; Dignitas Connubii, art. 35). 
- I AM WILLING  to provide any amplification or clarification of my testimony, to cooperate with an 
appointed Court Expert should a psychological evaluation or other expert analysis be requested (cf. can. 1680; 
Dignitas Connubii , art. 203), and to propose additional witnesses if the Tribunal deems further evidence 
necessary. 
- I AM AWARE of my right to be represented by an Advocate/Procurator (a certified Canon Lawyer with 
JCL or JCD degree), duly approved by the Tribunal, at my own expense, or to accept the assistance of one 
assigned by the Metropolitan Tribunal. I acknowledge that the same right is granted to the Respondent (cf. can. 
1481; Dignitas Connubii, arts. 101–104). 
STATEMENT 
- I INDICATE my full understanding that neither the Tribunal nor any of its officers can guarantee an 
affirmative decision, nor can any assurance be given regarding the duration or outcome of the process (cf. 
Mitis Iudex, art. 14 §1). 
- I ALSO AGREE that no wedding may be scheduled nor any marriage preparation undertaken until — 
and unless — I have received a final declaration of nullity of the marriage under examination and I have been 
declared canonically free to marry within the Catholic Church (cf. can. 1682 §2; Dignitas Connubii, art. 250).
