Abbreviated Process  
(Processus Brevior – MIDI cc. 1683 - 1687)  Ordinary Process 
(MIDI - cc. 1675 - 1682) 
This process requires the following: 
   
a) the signed Respondent’s Consent                     
(see attached form) at the time the petition 
is submitted OR submitted within 15 
(fifteen) working days upon notification 
of the process; 
 
b) the Petitioner’s Questionnaire provides           
sufficient information concerning the                
parties’ relationship at the time of and             
during the marriage, reasons for marrying, 
married life, and separation; 
 
c) corroborating testimony submitted within 
15 (fifteen) working days from witnesses 
who knew the parties at the time of and   
during the marriage; 
 
d) all pertinent documents submitted with the 
petition. 
 
In the abb reviated process, the diocesan 
Bishop, after considering the obser vations of 
the Defender of the Bond and in consultation 
with the instructor and assessor, will make 
the decision if he can arrive at moral certitude 
based on the proofs submitted. He can also 
refer the case to the ordinary process. 
This process requires the following: 
 
a) notifying the Respondent and assuring 
his/her right of defense. 
 
b) completing the Petitioner’s marital history, 
and if needed, with additional testimony; 
 
c) obtaining corroborating testimony from 
witnesses who knew the parties at the time 
of and during their marriage; 
 
d) collecting all pertinent documents related to 
the  parties and the marriage. 
 
In the ordinary process, the case decision                        
(final sentence)  will be communicated by the 
Judicial Vicar.   
 
  
DIRECTIONS FOR PETITION FOR FORMAL CASES OF NULLITY OF MARRIAGE 
To be completed by the Priest, Pastor, Deacon, or Pastoral Minister 
Pope Francis recently issued the apostolic letter, Mitis Iudex Dominus Iesus  (MIDI), reforming and simplifying the 
procedural norms regarding declaration of nullity of marriage.  These new norms took effect on December 8, 2015. 
  
The document also provides that the Judicial Vicar remains the ONLY person who will determine the process to be used.  
Once he has made that determination, the Judicial Vicar will inform the Parties of his decision. 
  
The Judicial Vicar can only recommend the abbreviated process if all the documents, the consent of the Respondent and 
testimonies from both Parties and witnesses have been received. 
 
 NO DATE for a future marriage may be set until the Tribunal has reached a final decision.
  
 NO ASSURANCE of a favorable decision can be given. 
  
 The Tribunal wants to assure the parties that the staff wishes to be of service to them in                        
accordance with Church law and the Church’s teaching on marriage.

STATEMENT OF TRIBUNAL POLICY 
 
The proceedings of the Tribunal of San Jose are exclusively religious in nature and are governed 
solely by the laws of the Roman Catholic Church.  These proceedings are not directed to such 
matters as property rights, custody of children, or other matters determined by the civil courts.  
The purpose of this investigation is to determine the status of the parties from the point of view 
of the Catholic Church and their freedom to enter a marriage which will be recognized as valid by 
the Catholic Church.  It is the Tribunal’s desire that the proceedings be a healing experience, 
conducted in a spirit of justice and compassion. 
 
Church law, which is concerned wi th the rights of both parties in the investigation, requires that 
the Tribunal seek sufficient information to make a responsible and just decision in this matter.  
The Tribunal gathers the testimony of the parties themselves and of the witnesses proposed b y 
the parties.  The Tribunal may also request, with proper authorization, information such as 
medical or psychological records. 
 
Because of the sensitive nature of the information gathered in this process and because the 
Tribunal wishes to promote a spirit  of charity, all information gathered in the course of the 
proceeding is considered confidential and is not made available to anyone except as authorized 
by Church law.  Under that law, it is the Presiding Judge of the Tribunal who has the authority to 
determine what information is to be given to the parties, balancing the rights of the parties, and 
the need for confidentiality.  Information is not made available to witnesses, to anyone acting in 
their behalf, or for any civil proceeding.  Witnesses may req uest that their testimony be withheld 
from the parties for a serious reason.  It is the policy of the Tribunal to disclose information only 
to duly authorized Tribunal officials as determined by Church law.  The goal is to afford the 
parties their rights a s well as to preserve the necessary confidentiality of all information 
gathered in the Tribunal’s process of investigation. 
 
I attest that I have read the above statement and agree to be bound by the policies explained 
therein.
