TRIBUNAL PRACTICES AND POLICIES 
Please Read Carefully, Then Initial & Sign the Affidavit on pg. 2 
 
In presenting a petition for a declaration of nullity of your marriage, you are asking that your marital stat us be clarified in the Roman 
Catholic Church.  The Tribunal is an ecclesiastical court of law, and its proceedings are exclusively religious in nature.  These proceedings 
are governed solely by the laws of the Roman Catholic Church.  The primary purpose of the Tribunal is to gather and sift through evidence 
in order to discover truth and serve justice.  It is also the Tribunal's desire that the proceedings be a healing experience conducted in a 
spirit of Christian understanding and compassion. 
 
Out of respe ct for the human dignity of persons and the sacred nature of marriage, Church tribunals are required to approach each 
marriage as valid, presuming that you and your former spouse both meant what you said and accomplished what you intended when you 
first exchanged vows.  There are numerous reasons or grounds of nullity that can overturn this presumption, but decisions in such cases 
are not arbitrary.  Declarations of nullity are not favors given to innocent or deserving parties.  They are statements about  the deepest 
spiritual truth of your former union, as best as any human judge can determine. Judges must have moral certitude, based on the evidence 
brought forward, that your former union was missing one of the elements essential for marriage as the Church teaches God intended it to 
be.  In the absence of such certitude, the judge must rule in the negative. 
 
 Concerning the Respondent (Your Former Spouse) 
 
Church Law requires that your former spouse be contacted and offered the opportunity to testify.  Make every effort to locate your former 
spouse so that he/she can be contacted by the Tribunal to participate in the process.  Understand that you need have no direc t contact 
with your former spouse, unless you feel it is in your own best interest to make contact and encourage a response. Your efforts to locate 
your former spouse should be documented and sent to the Tribunal if the search is not successful.  
 
 Concerning Witnesses 
 
Witnesses are necessary to process this petition for a declaration of marriage nul lity.  It is in your best interest to encourage their 
cooperation and timely response; please understand, however, that you are in no way to assist them in preparing their testimony. 
 
 Concerning Confidentiality 
 
Because of the sensitive nature of informat ion gathered in this process, and because the Tribunal wishes to promote a spirit of 
reconciliation, all the information gathered in the course of this investigation is considered confidential.  This informatio n is never made 
available except as required by Church Law for inspection by the Petitioner, the Respondent and officers of this Court. 
 
This information is never made available to the witnesses or anyone acting on their behalf, or in any civil legal proceedings.  In accord with 
applicable standards of professional accountability, reports from psychological counselors are always withheld from the Petitioner and the 
Respondent.  Witnesses may request that their testimony be withheld from the Petitioner and/or the Respondent for serious reason, such 
as avoidance of defamation of character, physical danger, family discord or scandal.   
  
The Presiding Judge may curtail the availability of testimony for serious reasons.  It is the policy of the Tribunal to discl ose this 
information to those duly authorized ecclesiastical officials or other ecclesiastical Tribunals when necessary for the resolution of the 
case. 
 
Concerning Expenses and Fees 
 
The ordinary fee for processing marriage nullity cases of this nature is $500.00.  This amount covers approximately 30% of total Tribunal 
costs for salaries, postage and telephone, office equipment and secretarial expenses.  The Diocese of Monterey subsidizes other ordinary 
Trib unal expenses.  However, in certain cases the judge may deem it necessary to employ the services of a professional in the behavioral 
sciences to assist in clarifying issues that may arise.  It is the responsibility of the Petitioner to defray these expenses.

 Marriage Case History 
 
 
 
 
It is important for the Church Tribunal to have an understanding of the marriage for which you are seeking a declaration 
of nullity. With this knowledge it can be decided which process is to be used or whether there is even sufficient evidence 
to begin a process.  The following OUTLINE is an aid to help you give the necessary information to the Tribunal. 
 
DIRECTIONS 
 
     1) Using the suggested OUTLINE, tell, in narrative form, the story of your marriage.  DO NOT MERELY ANSWER the 
questions.  Be as factual and complete as you can. 
 
     2) Your history should be at least approximately 4 to 5 double -spaced typewritten pages. Please sign and date it. We 
cannot accept unsigned case history.  
 
     3) Upon acceptance of your case, formal testimony will be sought. 
 
OUTLINE 
 
1.   BACKGROUND                   
 
Yourself:       
 Your name (maiden name if a woman), date and place of birth, nationality, religion. 
   Religion of your family;  number of your brothers and sisters; educational background and achievements, extent of 
religious education.  
       Family life: how you related to your parents, brothers and sisters; how your family got along with neighbors ; how 
your family practiced religion and fulfilled community and civic responsibilities.  
   History of mental/emotional illness in you or in your family; significant illnesses; counseling or psychotherapy; any 
special problems. 
 
Respondent:   Same information as above insofar as you know it. If you do not know it, please indicate why this is unknown. 
 
2.   MEETING AND COURTSHIP 
 
 Prior to meeting your former spouse, did you date other people; were there any other relationships which included 
engagement; if so, what were the causes of the break-offs;  
 How and when did you meet the Respondent; how old were each of you then; how long did you know each other 
before marriage; how often did you date; any problems between you while you were dating; whether you ever broke 
up and why;  
 How the subject of marriage came up; when did you decide to marry and how long were you engaged; reaction of 
each one's family to the plan to be married; any force involved; any opposition to the proposed marriage;  
 Attitude of your  self and Respondent toward ha ving children; toward use of sex during courtship; toward 
permanence in the proposed marriage; toward an exclusive relationship in marriage;  
 Whether either of you hesitated about getting married and why; whether either of you had doubts that the marriage 
would last, and why; did anyone advise against the marriage;

 Were contraceptive measures regularly used;  
 Was professional help sought in trying to resolve the problems? 
 
5.   FINAL SEPARATION

helpful to discuss this point with your parish priest).
