WHAT IS IT? The Briefer Process of marriage nullity, introduced by Mitis Iudex Dominus Iesus (2015), 
is an expedited canonical procedure used when the nullity of a marriage is evident and both parties 
consent to it. This process requires clear proofs, reliable witnesses, and full agreement of the 
spouses to proceed, aiming to reach a just and swift resolution without compromising the integrity of 
justice. 
 
- CONDITIONS FOR ADMITTANCE: A Briefer Process may be admitted only when ALL the following 
conditions are clearly and fully verified; otherwise, the petition shall ipso iure proceed as a Formal 
Ordinary Process in accordance with the norms of canons 1671–1679.  
 
Please review and check all that apply: 
 
 Sponsor Requirement  – The case must have a Sponsor, preferably the pastor of the parish.  
(Reference: Dignitas Connubii  art. 113 §2 — “The tribunal is to encourage the faithful to approach 
their proper pastor or another suitable person for assistance in preparing the libellus.”) 
 
 Form and Content of the Libellus  – The petition (libellus) must be presented briefly, fully, and 
clearly, expressing the object of the nullity, the facts on which it is based, and the supporting proofs.  
(Reference: can. 1504.) 
 
 Duration of Common Life  – The marriage should have been of brief duration, typically interpreted 
as up to five years of conjugal life. (Reference: Mitis Iudex Dominus Iesus, art. 14 §1 [“a brief conjugal 
cohabitation”]). 
 
 Witnesses – There must be at least two reliable witnesses who are willing and able to testify in 
person before the Tribunal. (Reference: can. 1526 §2, 2°). 
 
 Consent of Both Parties – Both spouses must consent to the petition and appear in person before 
the Court at a judicial hearing, during which their signatures and testimonies are collected.  
(References: cann. 1683–1687). 
 
 Participation of the Respondent  – The Respondent must expressly agree to the use of the Briefer 
Process and to personal participation. If the Respondent disagrees or refuses to appear, the case 
must proceed under the Formal Ordinary Process. (References: cann. 1683–1687). 
 
 Clear and Evident Circumstances of Nullity  – The case must present clear circumstances of 
things and persons that point evidently to the nullity of the marriage, supported by documentary or 
testimonial proofs submitted by the time of the judicial hearing.  (References: Mitis Iudex Dominus 
Iesus, art. 14 §1; can. 1683 §2). 
 
 Right of Defense – The right of defense must be fully safeguarded. If, at any stage, the Respondent 
retracts consent or new doubts arise regarding nullity, the case must automatically change to the 
Formal Ordinary Process. (Reference: can. 1620, 7°).
