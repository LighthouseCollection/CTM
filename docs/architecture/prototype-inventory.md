About — Ecclesiastical Council for Family Affairs
Office of the Ecclesiastical Council for Family Affairs
About the Council
Our Purpose
The Ecclesiastical Council for Family Affairs
"Marriage is one of the Sacraments of the Coptic Church — a union between one man and one woman for a life blessed by God."
The Coptic Orthodox Church holds marriage as one of its seven holy sacraments. Accordingly, the dissolution of an ecclesiastical marriage is not granted lightly. Unlike civil divorce — which in California may be obtained simply by declaring irreconcilable differences — the Church requires a formal canonical process before any permission to remarry may be considered.
The Ecclesiastical Council for Family Affairs is the canonical body established to carry out this process. It examines petitions for dissolution, hears testimony from both parties, reviews all relevant evidence and documentation, deliberates as a council, and issues a formal determination under the authority of the Presiding Bishop.
The two primary canonical grounds recognized by the Coptic Orthodox Church are adultery and a change of faith by one of the spouses. In rare circumstances, an annulment may also be declared. See the Determinations page for a full explanation of possible outcomes.
Before a case ever reaches the Council, the local parish priest works to resolve the marital situation. Often the couple meets with several priests and receives counsel. A referral to a professional marriage counselor is frequently suggested.
When there is an impasse — and certainly once a civil divorce has been granted — the case is referred to the Council based on the request of one or both spouses.
Composition
How the Council Is Formed
The Ecclesiastical Council for Family Affairs is comprised of several experienced priests chosen by the bishop. Each member brings both theological grounding in Coptic Orthodox canon law and the pastoral experience required to handle sensitive family matters with wisdom and care.
H.E. Metropolitan Serapion — Presiding Bishop
Bishop of Los Angeles, Southern California and Hawaii. All canonical decrees issued by the Council are signed by H.E. Metropolitan Serapion and carry his authority.
Council Members
Several ordained priests appointed by the bishop. The full council meets to deliberate and decide each case. One priest is assigned to investigate each individual case prior to the council meeting.
Investigating Priest
Each case is assigned to a specific council member who studies the situation in detail, holds individual interviews with both parties, and collects all relevant documentation before presenting findings to the full council.
Fr. Mauritius Mikhail — Secretary
The secretary is responsible for setting the agenda for each monthly meeting, managing all correspondence with petitioners, and coordinating the submission and review of case documentation.
Monthly Meetings
The Council typically meets once a month. Prior to the meeting, the secretary sets the agenda. Each assigned priest presents his findings — a discussion follows and a decision is reached.

2.1 Contacting the Church
Contact the church where the wedding will take place to secure a date and time. The paperwork process should begin at least two (2) months before the desired wedding date to give everyone plenty of time to prepare. Church availability varies by parish.
Confirm that the officiating priest will be available on the date and at the location you are considering.
All weddings in the diocese must be celebrated within a Coptic Orthodox Church. Weddings may not take place at homes, hotels, resorts, parks, beaches, or any venue other than a consecrated church.
2.2 Restricted Dates
The Church's liturgical calendar is rich with seasons of fasting, prayer, and celebration. Weddings are joyfully celebrated outside of the following sacred periods. Your priest can help you find the perfect date:
✕The Great Lent — the entire 55-day period leading up to Pascha (Easter), including Holy Week
✕Holy Week and Pascha — from Palm Sunday through Bright Saturday
✕Apostles' Fast — the period following Pentecost through the Feast of Saints Peter and Paul (July 12)
✕The Fast of the Virgin Mary (St. Mary) — August 7 through August 22
✕Advent (The Nativity Fast) — November 25 through January 6 (Christmas and Epiphany)
✕The Feast of the Epiphany — January 19 (Coptic calendar: 11 Toba)
✕The Fast of Nineveh — three days, approximately two weeks before Great Lent
✕Wednesdays and Fridays — weddings are not permitted on Wednesdays and Fridays, except during the Holy Fifty Days (the period between Resurrection and Pentecost)
2.3 Wedding Time
Discuss with your priest and the church the most appropriate time for the ceremony.
Keep in mind that the church may have other services or sacraments scheduled on the same day.
Plan backwards from your reception time, allowing sufficient time for the ceremony, photographs (up to 30 minutes inside the church), and travel to the reception venue.

3.3 Doctrinal Boundaries of the Wedding Ceremony
The Coptic Orthodox Church is a sacred religious institution founded upon the unchanging teachings of the Holy Bible, the Holy Apostolic Tradition, and the canonical doctrine of the Orthodox faith. The Church conducts its spiritual, sacramental, liturgical, and internal ecclesiastical life according to its own faith, canons, and theological teachings.
The Holy Sacrament of Matrimony in the Coptic Orthodox Church is not merely a civil ceremony, but a holy sacrament of the Church. It is strictly understood and celebrated as the sacred, lifelong union between one man and one woman before God.
Every part of the wedding ceremony, including the processional, the entrance of the bridal party, and all actions taking place within the church, must be consistent with the doctrine and sacramental practice of the Coptic Orthodox Church.
No portion of the ceremony, including the entrance or presentation of the bridal party, may include any arrangement, pairing, gesture, expression, ceremonial action, or representation that implies, suggests, represents, affirms, or celebrates a same-sex relationship or union. Any such arrangement is strictly prohibited within the church ceremony.
The Church reserves the right and responsibility to uphold the sanctity of its sacraments and to decline any practice, presentation, or participation that contradicts Orthodox Christian doctrine or the teachings of the Coptic Orthodox Church.

4.1 General Guidelines
The wedding is a sacred sacrament — the church is not a photography studio or a theater.
Please share the church's photography guidelines with your photographer and videographer before the wedding day so they can plan accordingly.
Photographers and videographers are encouraged to dress modestly and professionally (all black attire is recommended and looks great).
4.2 During the Ceremony
Photographers are asked to remain discreet and mindful of the congregation's view, allowing everyone to fully experience the sacrament.
The sanctuary (the area behind the iconostasis/altar screen) is a consecrated space and should be respected by all photographers and videographers.
All photography equipment should remain on the floor — please remind your team to respect the church furniture, icons, and sacred items by keeping a respectful distance.
A calm, reverent pace is appreciated — please walk rather than run inside the church.
The church's sacred atmosphere is best captured with natural and ambient lighting. Camera lights, floodlights, and additional lighting stands should be left outside for the ceremony.
4.3 After the Ceremony
You are welcome to take photographs inside the church after the ceremony — please plan to complete them within 30 minutes so you can enjoy your reception!
All posed photographs should take place in the nave (the main body of the church), keeping the sanctuary area reserved for worship.
Keep your photo poses joyful, loving, and reverent — fitting for the house of God.
If you would like to adjust any church furniture for photographs, please ask the priest for permission first.

Behavior and Etiquette Inside the Church
0 / 0
Acknowledged
The church is the house of God. The reverence and beauty of this sacred space will make your ceremony all the more meaningful. The following guidelines will help everyone honor the occasion together.
5.1 Arriving on Time
The entire wedding party — parents, bridesmaids, groomsmen, ushers, flower girls, ring bearers, and the bride and groom — should arrive at the agreed-upon time so everything flows smoothly.
Arriving on time for the ceremony is important to honor your guests, the priest, and the church's schedule.
The ceremony will begin at the scheduled time. Arriving promptly ensures the full, beautiful ceremony can be celebrated without being shortened.
Encourage your guests to arrive a few minutes early so they can settle in and be ready.
5.2 Conduct During the Ceremony
Please keep the church free of chewing gum — it helps keep this sacred space clean and beautiful.
Food and beverages (including water bottles) are best left outside the church.
The church and all church property are smoke-free, vape-free, and tobacco-free environments — including the parking lots.
The church is an alcohol-free and drug-free environment. Please ensure all members of the wedding party and guests honor this standard.
Kindly ask guests to set their cell phones to silent mode before entering the church so everyone can be fully present for the sacrament.
Parents are encouraged to keep young children close — if a little one needs a break, stepping outside briefly is perfectly fine.
The beauty of the ceremony speaks for itself — novelty signs (e.g., "Here Comes the Bride") are best saved for the reception or photo session outside the church.

Determinations — Ecclesiastical Council for Family Affairs
Office of the Ecclesiastical Council for Family Affairs
Determinations
Possible Outcomes
How the Council May Rule
After full investigation and deliberation, the Council may reach one of several determinations. Each case is decided individually on its merits.
Permission to Remarry Granted
If there is ample evidence against the other spouse, permission to remarry in the Coptic Church is granted to the innocent party — the individual not found to be at fault.
Deferred — Further Investigation Required
If there is not enough evidence or documentation to reach a decision, the investigating priest is asked to look further into specific aspects of the case. If the other individual cannot be met in person, a certified letter is sent requesting a response. If no response is received after a reasonable period, that person forfeits their right and the judgment proceeds.
No Permission Granted to Either Party
If both sides are found to have violated Church doctrine, no permission to remarry is granted to either party.
Asked to Reconsider
In some cases, the spouses are asked to reconsider reconciliation. Some try and succeed; others do not. The matter is then re-referred to the Council.
Annulment Declared
In rare cases, an annulment is declared — meaning the marriage is treated as if it never validly occurred under Church canon. The reasons for annulment are distinct from those for dissolution. See the Annulment Reasons section below.
Annulment
Reasons for a Declaration of Annulment
Annulment is distinct from dissolution. It declares that the marriage lacked canonical validity from its very beginning — due to fraud, misrepresentation, or a disqualifying condition that was concealed from the other party at the time of marriage. The following reasons are recognized:
Prior undisclosed marriage
One party was already married — in Egypt or elsewhere — at the time of the Coptic Orthodox wedding. Their clearance was therefore fraudulent, and the subsequent marriage is considered canonically invalid.
Undisclosed serious physical illness
A spouse had a significant physical condition prior to marriage — such as cancer, a serious heart condition, or tuberculosis — that was known to them but not disclosed to the other party before the marriage took place.
Misrepresentation of virginity
A man or woman was represented as a virgin prior to marriage while he or she was not. This reason requires careful examination and is subject to the discretion of the Council.
Undisclosed mental illness
A pre-existing mental health condition — such as schizophrenia, obsessive behavior, or other significant psychological disorder — was known prior to marriage but was not disclosed to the other party.
Misrepresentation of status or circumstances
One party misrepresented their financial status, professional position, or other significant circumstances in a way that directly affected the decision to marry.
Inability to consummate the marriage
Either for medical or psychological reasons, the parties were never able to consummate the marriage.
Prior knowledge of inability to conceive
The woman was aware prior to the marriage that she was unable to conceive and bear children, and this was not disclosed to the other party.

FAQ — Ecclesiastical Council for Family Affairs
Office of the Ecclesiastical Council for Family Affairs
Frequently Asked Questions
Your Questions Answered
Is a civil divorce required before filing a petition?
Yes. A civil divorce must be finalized before the Council can accept a petition. A certified copy of the civil divorce decree must be included with the application. The Council does not review cases in which a civil divorce has not yet been granted.
How long does the process take?
The Council meets once a month. The duration depends on the complexity of the case, the availability and cooperation of both parties, and how promptly documentation is submitted. Most cases are resolved within several months to a year from the date a complete petition is received.
What are the canonical grounds for dissolution?
The two primary canonical grounds recognized by the Coptic Orthodox Church are adultery and a change of faith by one of the spouses. In certain circumstances, an annulment may also be declared.
What is the difference between dissolution and annulment?
A dissolution ends a marriage that was canonically valid. An annulment declares that the marriage never validly existed from the beginning — due to fraud, misrepresentation, or a disqualifying condition that was concealed at the time of marriage. Annulments are rare and require substantial evidence.
What are the grounds for an annulment?
Recognized grounds include: prior undisclosed marriage; undisclosed serious physical illness; misrepresentation of virginity; undisclosed pre-existing mental illness; misrepresentation of financial status or circumstances; inability to consummate the marriage; and prior knowledge of inability to conceive.
Does the respondent have to cooperate?
Participation by the respondent is encouraged and beneficial, but it is not required. The assigned priest will make every effort to hear from both parties. If the respondent cannot be reached, a certified letter is sent requesting their input.
What happens if the respondent does not respond?
A certified letter is sent to the respondent requesting clarification or a response. If no response is received after a reasonable period, the respondent forfeits their right to be heard and the judgment is given in favor of the petitioner.
Can both parties be denied permission to remarry?
Yes. If both parties are found to have violated Church doctrine, the Council may deny permission to remarry to both individuals. No determination automatically favors the petitioner — each case is decided on the merits of the evidence presented.
Can I remarry in the Church after a decree is issued?
It depends on the decree. Permission to remarry is generally granted to the innocent party. The party found at fault may be given permission under strict conditions, or denied entirely. In the case of an annulment, the innocent party is granted outright permission to remarry.
What is the $200 fee for?
The $200 fee covers the administrative costs of processing the petition — including case management, correspondence, scheduling, and documentation. The fee is non-refundable. No petition will be refused solely on the basis of financial hardship; please contact the office confidentially to discuss your situation.
Is everything confidential?
Absolutely. All proceedings, testimony, and documentation are held in strict confidence. Only the council members and authorized staff have access to case materials. Nothing is shared with the congregation, parish clergy, or any outside party.
How are financial disputes handled?
Financial matters are handled entirely through the civil courts — not through the Church or the Council. The Ecclesiastical Council for Family Affairs has no jurisdiction over the distribution of assets, property, or financial obligations.
Will the Council accept evidence obtained illegally?
No. The Council will hold any evidence or documentation obtained through illegal means as inadmissible and will not consider such information in the examination of a case.
Will the Council hear testimony from children?
No. To protect the family unit and the wellbeing of children, the Council will not hear the testimony of children under the age of 16 — for or against either party involved in the case.
Who bears the burden of proof?
The applicant bears the burden of proof. It is the responsibility of the petitioner to provide sufficient evidence and documentation to substantiate the grounds upon which the petition is based.
Will the Council use anything I disclosed in Holy Confession?
Absolutely not. The Church in its entirety, and the Ecclesiastical Council specifically, will never compromise the sanctity and confidence of the Sacrament of Confession and Repentance. Anything disclosed in confession is and will always remain inviolable.
I have been separated for 3 or 5 years — can I get a dissolution or annulment?
No. Length of separation is not a canonical ground for dissolution or annulment in the Coptic Orthodox Church. A period of separation alone — regardless of whether children are involved — does not constitute grounds. Recognized grounds are listed on the Determinations page.

### Visible prototype text

The Process — Ecclesiastical Council for Family Affairs
Office of the Ecclesiastical Council for Family Affairs
The Canonical Process
Step by Step
How a Case Proceeds
The process is deliberate and thorough. The Council meets monthly and approaches each case with the care, confidentiality, and canonical integrity it deserves.
Step 1
Civil Divorce Required
A civil divorce must be finalized before a petition can be submitted to the Council. A certified copy of the civil divorce decree must accompany the application.
Step 2
Submit a Petition & Documents
A formal petition is submitted together with all required documentation. The application consists of nine parts, including personal information, marriage details, a case summary, supporting documents, and a declaration of veracity.
Step 3
Case Assignment
The case is assigned to a specific council member who will be responsible for the full investigation and presentation of findings to the council.
Step 4
Investigation & Hearings
That priest studies the situation in full detail, holds individual interviews with both parties, and collects all relevant documentation prior to presenting the case to the full council.
Step 5
Council Meeting & Presentation
The Council meets monthly. The assigned priest presents his findings. A full discussion follows among all council members. All proceedings, testimony, and documentation are held in the strictest confidence — nothing is shared with the congregation, parish clergy, or any outside party.
Step 6
Determination & Decree Issued
The council reaches a determination — this may include permission to remarry, denial, deferral for further investigation, or a declaration of annulment. See the Determinations page for the full range of possible outcomes. A formal canonical decree is drafted, signed by H.E. Metropolitan Serapion, and delivered to both parties. The decree is permanently recorded in the Council's registry.

In the Name of the Father, the Son, and the Holy Spirit, One God. Amen.
The Clerical Tribunal of the Coptic Orthodox Diocese of Los Angeles, having been duly convened under the authority of His Eminence Metropolitan Serapion, and having received and examined the petition of Jessica Marie O'Brien against Patrick James O'Brien regarding the dissolution of their ecclesiastical marriage, hereby issues the following decree.
Findings of Fact
Approved
The Tribunal finds the following facts established through testimony and evidence presented:
1. The parties were joined in holy matrimony on June 12, 2010 at St. Mark Coptic Orthodox Church, Los Angeles, officiated by Fr. Antony Attallah.
2. The respondent, Patrick James O'Brien, engaged in an extramarital relationship beginning approximately March 2024, as admitted in his testimony of April 8, 2025.
3. The applicant discovered the infidelity in September 2024 and sought pastoral counsel from Fr. Youhana Ibrahim.
4. Reconciliation was attempted through three sessions of marriage counseling between August and October 2024. The respondent declined to continue after the third session.
5. Fr. Youhana Ibrahim submitted a written statement on October 28, 2024 confirming that all pastoral avenues for reconciliation had been exhausted.
6. The civil divorce was finalized on January 6, 2025 at the Los Angeles Superior Court.
Canonical Basis
Approved
The petition is brought on the ground of infidelity, which is recognized as a valid ground for the dissolution of an ecclesiastical marriage under the canonical tradition of the Coptic Orthodox Church, in accordance with the Lord's teaching in the Gospel of St. Matthew (19:9).
Decree Statement
Approved
Having carefully examined the testimony of both parties, the documentary evidence presented, the written statement of Fr. Youhana Ibrahim confirming the exhaustion of all reconciliation efforts, and the requisition report prepared by Fr. Gawargious Kotta, the Tribunal hereby grants the petition for the dissolution of the ecclesiastical marriage between Jessica Marie O'Brien and Patrick James O'Brien.
The respondent's admission of the extramarital relationship during testimony, together with the corroborating evidence, satisfies the evidentiary standard required under canonical procedure. The Tribunal is satisfied that the ground of infidelity has been established and that all due pastoral and procedural steps were followed prior to this decree.
Conditions & Directives
Approved
1. The applicant, Jessica Marie O'Brien, is permitted to remarry within the Coptic Orthodox Church following a period of spiritual preparation as directed by her Father of Confession.
2. The respondent, Patrick James O'Brien, shall undergo a period of repentance and spiritual counseling with his Father of Confession, Fr. Antony Attallah, before any future marriage within the Church may be considered. The duration and nature of this period shall be determined by Fr. Antony Attallah in consultation with His Eminence.
3. Both parties are encouraged to continue to prioritize the spiritual welfare and upbringing of their children within the Orthodox faith.
